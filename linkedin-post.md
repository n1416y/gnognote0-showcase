# LinkedIn introduction draft

## Recommended version (about 175 words)

The difficult part isn’t getting an AI to act. It’s knowing when it shouldn’t.

I started gnognote0 to explore what it takes to build AI systems that can do more than respond: remember context, identify useful work, make bounded decisions, recover when interrupted, and show clear evidence of what happened. The challenge is balancing initiative with accountability. A system that can act also needs a credible way to defer, decline, and explain its limits.

One concrete thread is G0, an offline hurricane-preparedness and resilience knowledge project. It has pushed me to think about provenance, stale information, and how to make useful material understandable when connectivity is limited.

The longer-term creative ambition is an experience where people can observe an AI’s evolving work and influence some small choices without taking control of its purpose. That remains an experiment, not a launched product.

I’ve shaped the product direction, architecture, engineering priorities, and governance, while using AI-assisted coding tools to help implement and test the work. My background in technology strategy, procurement, and AI governance keeps bringing me back to the same question: how do we make capability useful and accountable at the same time?
